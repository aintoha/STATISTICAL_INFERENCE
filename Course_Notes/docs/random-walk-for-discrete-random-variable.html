6 Random Walk for Discrete Random Variable

The mathematical concept of the “drunken walk” - formally known as the random walk—was developed independently at the turn of the 20th century by thinkers across finance, biology, and physics attempting to model unpredictable behavior.

Go to this link: https://aintoha.github.io/DrawCards/ The simulator uses a deck of 52 playing cards (drawn with replacement) to determine the direction of each step. The rules are simple:

  • Red Cards (Hearts / Diamonds): Take +1 step forward (Right)

  • Black Cards (Spades / Clubs): Take -1 step backward (Left)

Task: Set the ‘Maximum Draws’ to 5. Run the simulation 10 times and record your final landing positions. Bring these 10 results to class!

Please answer the following questions on this sheet based on your experience with the simulator.

  1. If you set the Maximum Draws to exactly 5, how many unique paths (combinations of Red and Black cards) are possible?

  2. In a 5-draw game, what is the exact probability of ending up at position +3? Write your answer as a fraction.

  3. In that same 5-draw game, what is the probability of ending at position +3? (Hint: List out the different ways you can draw two Red cards and one Black card).

  4. If you take exactly 5 draws, is it possible to land on position +1 or +3? Why or why not?

  5. Let the random variable X represent your final position after exactly 5 draws. Construct the PMF table of X.

  6. What is the probability that your final position X is strictly greater than 3?

  7. What is the probability that your final position X is less than or equal to 4?

  8. Suppose you peek at your very first card and see it is Red. Given this condition, what is the probability that your final position after all 5 draws will be exactly +2

6.0.1 Application of Random Walk

  • Drawing a random card from the deck is mathematically identical to clicking the “Take Step” button. Both trigger a single, independent 50/50 random event that dictates the next move.

  • The Red Card vs. The Green Upward Line

    • Drawing a Red Card to gain $10 is the same as a positive market event.

    • On the simulator’s Tree Preview, this is represented by the green line moving diagonally upward, increasing the asset’s value by whatever number you typed into the “Volatility Step” box.

  • The Black Card vs. The Red Downward Line

    • Drawing a Black Card to lose $10 translates to a negative market shock.

    • On the visual tree, this is the red line moving diagonally downward to a lower price node.

  • The 4 Draws vs. The Number of Weeks

    • Playing a game with 4 card draws is identical to setting the simulator’s “Number of Weeks” to 4.

    • Each vertical column of circles on the grid represents the exact state of the market after one draw.

Questions

  1. If you set the Time Horizon to exactly 4 weeks, how many unique price paths (combinations of Up and Down weeks) are possible?

  2. In this 4-week market, what is the exact probability of the stock ending at exactly $12? Write your answer as a fraction.

  3. In this same 4-week market, what is the probability of ending exactly where you started, at $10? (Hint: List out the different path combinations where the market has exactly two Up weeks and two Down weeks).

  4. If you run the simulation for exactly 4 weeks, is it possible for the final stock price to land on exactly $11 or $13? Why or why not?

  5. Let the random variable \(X\) represent the final stock price after exactly 4 weeks. Construct the complete Probability Mass Function (PMF) table for \(X\).

  6. What is the probability that your final stock price \(X\) is strictly greater than $10?

  7. What is the probability that your final stock price \(X\) is less than or equal to $8?

  8. Suppose you watch the market in Week 1 and see the price go UP to $11. Given this new information, what is the exact probability that your final price after all 4 weeks will end up back at $10?

  9. The 4-week month is over, and you see that the final stock price is exactly $10. Given this final outcome, what is the exact probability that the market went UP during Week 1?

  10. Imagine instead that the month is over and the final stock price is exactly $12. Given this final outcome, what is the exact probability that the market went DOWN during Week 1? (Hint: Write out the specific paths that lead to a $12 final price, and look at how they start).
